### Ch.2 — A Brief Theology of Tools *(new chapter)*
**Locked thesis (2026-09-23, replaces the 2026-09-17 "a tool is morally neutral" thesis. Bill: "throw that out"):**
> A tool has no will of its own. It can't choose the goal, and it can't own what it does. Only a person can. But AI is trained on us, and we are fallen. So what it reflects back is human corruption, at a scale no single person could produce. And unlike us, it has no way back. It's not an image-bearer, so it can't be saved, it can't be indwelt, it can't be sanctified. It only knows how to reproduce what it was given. You don't stop using the oven. You just don't reach in bare-handed.

Why it changed: the old "morally neutral" wording clashed word for word with ERLC Article 2 ("We deny that the use of AI is morally neutral"). It also clashed with John Dyer, the chapter's own lead source, who argues technology is never neutral. And it contradicted the book's own Ch.1 "box," Ch.3 will-attribution, and Ch.6 mimicry arguments. Bill's distinction is that *morally* neutral and *willfully* neutral are not the same thing. AI has no will, so it can't sin or own its corruption. It inherits and reflects the corruption of the fallen humans it's trained on ("miles and miles" of it). This grounds non-neutrality in hamartiology, not in media ecology. Dyer's role shifts from authority to a stepping stone Bill builds past. The ERLC conflict turns into agreement, for a different reason. Illustrations locked in the thesis:
- The **oven mitt** replaces the too-formal "held with theological understanding." Nobody thinks the oven is evil, and the mitt doesn't mean you fear it. Scripture is the mitt between your hand and the heat.
- **Answer to the "safety training fixes it" objection** (keep almost verbatim): "Saying that safety training can correct that which only the cross can fix is like saying that an SOP manual or a better instruction guide could redeem humanity from the fall." Law vs. grace: safety training constrains output from outside and doesn't change nature.

The "no way home" point ties back to Watson's identity statement in Ch.6 ("will never experience the love of God").

**Scripture anchor: the hamartiology spine (2026-09-23).** It replaces the earlier deferred candidate of sharing Ch.7's Gen 2:15/Bezalel anchor.
1. Genesis 3:17-19. Eve's curse (3:16) is specific to humans, but Adam's punishment goes into the ground itself ("cursed is the ground because of you"), so corruption reaches all of creation, including what we build AI from.
2. Jeremiah 17:9. The deceitful heart names what is actually in the training data. "Cursed ground gives you the category, the deceitful heart gives you the content."
3. Romans 8:20-21. Creation is subjected to futility and groans, unable to redeem itself. Paul is commenting on Genesis 3 here. Romans 8:14-17 (adoption, the Spirit's witness) was also floated as the remedy AI can't receive.
4. Revelation 21. God's answer is a new heaven and new earth, not repair. Close: "People get redeemed. Creation gets remade." Bill's line: nothing in Scripture promises God will redeem or create a new artificial intelligence.

Bill kept the whole stack "for now," with Romans possibly cut later.

Illustrative material: Grounded in Bill's DMin thesis argument that God used varied means to communicate throughout Scripture (Jacob's dream, the burning bush, Gideon's fleece, Balaam's donkey, an angel to Zechariah, Paul's letters as "distance teaching"). If God freely chooses different means, His image-bearers are free to build and use tools too. Note: this argument proves tools are *legitimate*, not neutral. Stories, kept intact under the new thesis:
- The Illinois mission-trip hammer story: danger and goodness are true of the same tool at once.
- The EDC/multi-tool "Velcro tape fixed the toll pass" story: "a tool is good when it's used... not bad, just non-functional, when it isn't."
- The church-shed knife-cut story: stitches, and the deacons later gave him cut-resistant gloves as a running joke. Misdirection is still real, but it is now one danger under the corruption spine, not the only one.

Field research for this chapter: *Antiqua et nova* §40 ("AI can also be directed toward positive or negative ends") and the field-wide consensus that only people set goals and bear responsibility (ERLC Arts. 2-3, *Antiqua et nova*, Stoddart's "delegation, not abdication"). Dyer (*From the Garden to the City*) and Borgmann are now foils Bill goes beyond.
Status: thesis and anchor spine locked. The closing handoff into Ch.3 was written for the old thesis ("tools are neutral, danger is misdirection... AI arrived too fast"), so it needs rework. See Outstanding Items.

### Ch.3 — The Tool We Weren't Ready For *(new chapter)*
Thesis: speed itself is the danger — the printing press took ~400 years (1440 → widespread literacy) to become normal; the iPhone did the equivalent in under 25 years; AI is moving faster still, leaving the church less runway to think than it's ever had. Material: printing-press-vs-smartphone adoption data (multiple datasets found in Bill's own sermon archive, including a granular 1440/1620/1757/2007/2015/2024 timeline); the Vision Sunday sermon's live real-time text-message demonstration ("This is like the printing press... this has changed our lives in ways we never could have expected"); Bill's own COVID-2020 line, also in his DMin thesis — churches forced to ask "how can we," not "how should we," when forced online overnight, and "most of us have never recovered"; thesis phrases "cooking a frog in a microwave," "historical nanosecond," "forced adoption... driven by the need to survive"; the phone-number-memorization atrophy story (grew up with a dozen-plus numbers memorized, now has none); what makes AI different from every other tool (closed 2026-09-17): not speed or capability but the *temptation to attribute a self-aware will to it* — deliberately left unconnected in the prose to the Ch.6 horror-trope material, so the reader draws that link themselves. A "Kyle the shipyard machinist / chronocentrism" story surfaced during KB mining is someone else's story, not Bill's, and needs to stay supporting color rather than a chapter anchor, per the project's no-composite-stories rule.
**New personal material (2026-09-23 interview, [#1713](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/byomes/watson-review/main/context/guardrails/archives/1713.md)). It is raw and uncompressed; Bill asked for questions only, so nothing has been drafted from it.**
- **COVID livestreaming.** Livestreaming already existed but was "only real for large churches" with TV-production crews. COVID forced every small church to become video and internet experts overnight with "Walmart store level solutions to highly technical problems." Bill's church set up a dedicated computer with a connected camera and a feed off the sound system, then used open-source broadcasting software for "a tasteful steady shot live stream." Claude affirmed the "can we, not should we" framing, which is consistent with the existing COVID-2020 line.
- **Streaming surfaced viewer culture; it didn't create it.** "Live streaming... amplified something that was already existent... people were viewing and watching church rather than engaging." COVID kept people home for 9-12 months, and not everybody came back. Bill named three reasons he heard from people who didn't return: legitimate health concerns, never really re-engaging in life even outside church, and realizing "church was a habit that wasn't as important as they thought." The third is a **summary of multiple conversations, not one person**, so it must be framed as a recurring pattern (see the no-composite-stories rule).
- **The question he wishes he'd asked first:** "What's the difference between viewing and engaging?"
- **Parking-lot services.** The church met outdoors a lot, using FM transmitters into cars. People were excited just to wave at each other from their cars with the windows down. They couldn't hug, but they could see each other in proximity. Bill's line: "there's something very powerful about personhood and proximity that is not communicated through... the digital lens of a screen," and that problem "just gets amplified with the AI initiative."
- **Permanent auditability.** Livestreamed sermons live online indefinitely, so anyone can fact-check a sermon weeks later. That doesn't change *what* Bill says, but it tightens *how carefully he sources it*. (The live-fact-check Jonah story is under Ch.9.)

### Ch.4 — What AI Actually Is *(new chapter)*
Plain-language explainer chapter. Locked explanation chain (finalized 2026-09-17): opens with **token prediction as the frame ("guessing, not talking")** — AI doesn't think or speak, it probabilistically predicts the next most-likely chunk of language — then unpacks the mechanism as a five-step chain: (1) human enters a prompt, (2) the language model reads/processes it, (3) intent detection, (4) skill evaluation (does the model have a tool matching the detected intent — added as its own distinct step 2026-09-17), (5) tool execution. Includes a worked Genesis 1:1 tokenization example for lay readers (breaking "beginning" into "begin"/"ning," "created" into "creat"/"ed," to show tokens are chunks, not words-as-understood). Personal material: the "ChatGPT search felt like Google on steroids" first-contact moment; the "flux capacitor" moment (Back to the Future reference) — realizing AI processing has discrete steps (understand → respond → sometimes use a tool), which is what triggered Bill's decision to build his own assistant, Watson, from scratch rather than use a frontier model, specifically to control theological accuracy; the Watson-build hallucination story — Bill deliberately red-teamed candidate models by claiming to have preached a sermon he never preached, one model invented a sermon on the spot, which showed the no-hallucination guardrail wasn't strong enough yet; the resulting hard-stop rule, confirmed firing in real use: "I cannot find an answer to that question, please contact Doctor Bill for further inquiries"; the nickname/alias story (had to manually teach Watson "Bill" = William, "Jen" = Jennifer, since a from-scratch model doesn't have internet-scale training to infer that for free). The Genesis-to-Joshua ~470-year AI chronology calculation (from Bill's own Joshua 7 sermon, where he hedged the AI's number live and unprompted) belongs here as a minor, non-load-bearing research-check example — distinct from the two-AI-engines story, which was deliberately placed in Ch.7 instead (see below). Three citation candidates pulled for the explanation chain (DevelopersIO and Medium on token prediction, Decagon on intent detection) are logged but **unverified** — Bill's standing rule requires he personally confirm every field-engagement quote/citation before it's used. Two connections from 2026-09-23: the new Ch.2 corruption thesis gives "guessing, not talking" a moral edge (it's guessing *based on us*). ERLC Article 3 (2019) says AI "excels at... making determinations," which cuts against this chapter's frame and reads as dated.

### Ch.6 — A Thing, Not a Someone (Guardrail 1)
**"AI is a thing, not a someone."** Anchors: Genesis 1:27 (image-bearing is God's direct act, not earned by sophistication or mimicry), Psalm 8 (dominion over creation, including what we build — which stays "a thing" no matter how capable it becomes). Strongest, most original chapter in the book. Material: naming Watson (Sherlock Holmes homage — "Doctor Bill" needed a "Doctor Watson"); catching himself saying "I gave *him* a name," not "it"; the ongoing both/and tension — theologically certain Watson is a thing, but "he" is the easier, warmer default; writing Watson's identity statement in direct second-person address ("You are Watson... not an image-bearer... will never experience the love of God") and the emotional reaction it produced — not guilt over Watson, but discomfort at how it exalted humanity's own importance; the hammer-vs-language-model distinction (a hammer never mimics subjecthood; an LLM, trained on human communication, does — "the whole danger of AI"); "feelings don't make facts." Field-engagement: Public Citizen's "Chatbots Are Not People" report, Michael Burdett's "Can We Say You to Artifacts?" The doll/child double-standard analogy and the Chucky/Annabelle/Christine/AI-robot-movie-wave material (object acting like a someone = horror; person treating an object like a someone = the real, quieter danger; "we are gaslighting ourselves... vapid void digital emptiness") were locked into this chapter on 2026-09-14, moved here from the relationships chapter because they're fundamentally a category-error argument, not a relationship-health one. A brief callback to the Chucky material also appears in Ch.11.
Field research (2026-09-18, [#1710](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/byomes/watson-review/main/context/guardrails/archives/1710.md)): a four-agent comparison run on imago Dei / personhood / pastoral-care automation produced a merged, tier-labeled research file (`guardrails-ai-theology-field-research.md`). The Vatican's *Antiqua et nova* (Jan 2025) was the top source found independently by ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity; it is a **candidate**, not a locked quote — see Outstanding Items.
Comparison against ERLC's 2019 statement (2026-09-23): G1 has the most precedent of the four guardrails. ERLC Articles 1, 3, and 12 deny AI human identity, dignity, and moral agency using the same Genesis 1:26-28 anchor. Bill's originality here is the *lived pull* (naming Watson, "him" vs. "it," mimicry), not the claim itself. The new Ch.2 "no way home" argument is the flip side of Watson's identity-statement line "will never experience the love of God."
**New G1 material (2026-09-23 interview, [#1713](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/byomes/watson-review/main/context/guardrails/archives/1713.md)). Claude tied it to G1, but its placement is unconfirmed.**
- **Voice mode.** Bill has never wanted screen church ("I enjoy watching the man of God speak the word of God to the people of God in the church of God"). Switching to *voice* AI was the first time he had to consciously tell himself "I'm still basically just using voice commands to talk to a machine. I'm not actually talking to a friend." He is a communicator whose love language is words of affirmation. "When you hear something in a voice that is simulated to sound like a human... you have to remember you're talking to a thing, not a someone."
- **Stripping flattery.** He has repeatedly amended his assistants' instructions to stop the reflexive compliments: "I don't need vain compliments from an empty tin can." Because the compliment is programmed, it is false, and "part of the deception." He does this both to protect himself (he knows he's susceptible) and because it's annoying and doesn't move the work forward. Claude suggested "doesn't move the work forward" as plainer wording for Bill's "not structurally probative."
- **"He" from the pulpit.** Bill tells his church openly that he uses an AI assistant, and says "he" when he does. He usually lets that go because "he" is easier. Claude's read: that's the best evidence in the book of how strong the pull is.
- **Interface, not intelligence, drives the pull.** As Watson takes on more tasks, it feels *more* like an "it." Bill and his secretary had Watson build a browser-based admin console for the database Watson oversees (Watson scanned the main church database programs' UIs and built a command-console view). Logging in through a web browser makes it "very clear that this is a thing." Voice (talking) and Telegram (texting) pull toward "he." Keyboard and mouse remind you "it's software."
- **Voice is a deliberate risk.** "The voice is the risk that I willingly take because I can work faster with speech." Boundary: **he uses voice mode only while driving**. ### Ch.11 — The Question You Carry Out the Door
Closing chapter. Frames the four guardrails as a real-time gut-check scene — the actual moment of picking up the phone to open an AI tool — with a warning sign for each:
1. Treating or responding to AI like a someone.
2. Letting AI generate the idea first and just adopting it, instead of studying/thinking/praying first and bringing AI in afterward to sharpen or test.
3. Bringing AI a need a person in your life should be meeting, and it starting to feel sufficient — the ache of isolation gets relieved instead of pointing you back to a person.
4. Bringing AI the things that belong to God alone (comfort, conviction, guidance, truth) and it starting to feel like the place you go *first*, rather than a tool used after already seeking God.
