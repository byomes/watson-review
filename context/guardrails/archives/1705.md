Notice most of these are just whole words. That's actually the deceptive part. On simple, common sentences like this one, tokens often line up neatly with words, so it looks like the AI is reading the way you do. It's on rarer words, invented words, or unusual spellings that you'd see a word get sliced into pieces, something like "beginning" splitting into "begin" and "ning." That's when the illusion cracks and you can see it's really just predicting chunks, not grasping meaning the way a person reading Genesis 1:1 does.

Tokenized, with a few words split into pieces so the difference is visible:

See what happened there. "Beginning" became two chunks, "begin" and "ning." "Created" became "creat" and "ed." "Heavens" became "heaven" and "s." Short, common words like "in," "the," "God," "and," "earth" stayed whole because the model has seen them so often they get their own single token.

That's the whole point for your readers: the AI isn't reading "beginning" as one meaningful word the way we do. It's stacking fragments it's seen a billion times before, then guessing which fragment comes next. It never touches the idea of a beginning at all.
